{%- comment -%} The one place posts get ordered. Assigns `posts`, newest first; every template that lists posts includes this instead of sorting for itself. Every post carries `year:`. A post that wants day precision — an essay, say — adds `posted: 2024-03-01`, and that is what the templates show. Neither field is Jekyll's `date:`, and that is deliberate. Jekyll invents a `date` for any document that lacks one, silently filling it with the file's modification time, so `post.date` is always truthy, always displayable, and always wrong for undated work. `year` and `posted` are only ever set by hand, so they can be tested and trusted. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign years = site.entries | group_by: "year" | sort: "name" | reverse -%} {%- assign posts = "" | split: "" -%} {%- for year in years -%} {%- assign within = year.items | sort: "posted", "last" | reverse -%} {%- assign posts = posts | concat: within -%} {%- endfor -%}
